In short

    The three model and runtime combinations

    There are two different neural networks. The old one ("honest tench") is in both DLLs, byte for byte the same weights. The new "small" network is only in 310.9.16 and is its default. So there are three combinations to compare.

    GPU memory by working scale

    Model cost by working resolution

    GPU time of the NR model alone (the fork's timer around NVIDIA's evaluate call), 4K output, the frozen tavern frame, two shuffled rounds of about 14 s each. The game's own frame rate is not part of this. The cost is almost exactly a fixed part plus a part proportional to the number of pixels the model works on.

    What a fixed cost in milliseconds does to fps

    fps = 1000 / frame time, so the curve is steep at high fps and flat at low fps. Adding the same few milliseconds moves a 120 fps frame much further down the curve than a 30 fps frame. That is why a model does not cost a fixed percentage of fps: its cost in ms is fixed, its cost in fps depends on where you start. Each coloured step starts at a frame rate without NR, goes right by the model's fixed cost in ms (the same length everywhere), then drops to the curve: that drop is the fps you lose, and it is much bigger at 120 fps than at 30 fps.

    With DLSS frame generation the model runs once per rendered frame, and every rendered frame is presented together with one generated frame, so presented fps is about 2 × rendered fps (the generated frame is interpolated from NR's output). The table assumes that and nothing else; real games add small overheads.

    Every setting and its effect

    Change against the default of the same runtime, all at working scale 1.0, mean |difference| in 8-bit units. The noise floor is the "Default again" row: anything at or near it is no visible change. Detail difference = the fine (high-pass) part of NR's edit that differs from the default's edit, as a fraction of the default's fine edit (0 = the same fine edit). Shift = mean brightness change, Sat = mean saturation change, Contrast = change of the luminance spread. Flicker = mean frame-to-frame change in the frozen scene (lower is calmer).

    ControlMask: per-pixel control of NR (new, research build r8)

    NVIDIA's runtime accepts a per-pixel mask, DLSSNR.ControlMask: red = how much of NR's result is used (intensity), green = the local tone input, blue = the local structure input. The fork never sent one. The research build now builds it on the GPU every frame from depth, brightness and motion, at the model's working size, just before NVIDIA's evaluate. Cost: about +0.13 ms at 4K (measured, model timer, small model). All pictures: The Witcher 3, 310.9.16 small model, same frozen frame per scene.
